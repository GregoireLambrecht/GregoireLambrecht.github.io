About

I am a PhD student at the Center for Data Science, in the joint program between New York University and NYU Shanghai, advised by Mathieu Laurière. I work on mean field games, with a focus on inverse problems. The goal is to observe large populations of rational individuals and infer their objectives and the key properties of their environment. My main tools are control theory, reinforcement learning and, more broadly, machine learning.

Selected publications

  • Population-Aware Imitation Learning in Mean-field Games with Common Noise
    G. Lambrecht, M. Laurière — International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML), 2026
    We study how common noise affects imitation learning in mean field games, and show that ignoring it during learning degrades the performance of the learned policy.
    Paper ICML
  • Inverse Problems for Costs and Controls in LQG MFGs via Mean Field Trajectories
    G. Lambrecht, M. Laurière — IEEE Conference on Decision and Control (CDC), 2026 (to appear)
    We study the identifiability of the cost parameters of linear-quadratic-Gaussian (LQG) mean field games, and propose a semidefinite program (SDP) that recovers the costs from the observed first and second moments of the population trajectory.
    Paper
